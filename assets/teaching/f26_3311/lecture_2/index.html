Lecture 2: Symmetries of roots of unity

September 3, 2026 · Multiplicative structure and rigid motions

Lecture 2

Two kinds of symmetry

The fifth roots of unity carry more than one kind of structure. They form a set with multiplication, and geometrically they are the vertices of a regular pentagon. A permutation can preserve one of these structures without preserving the other.

Multiplicative symmetry

The four maps $\sigma_k(\zeta_5^j)=\zeta_5^{kj}$, for $k=1,2,3,4$, are exactly the bijections of the fifth roots that preserve multiplication. In particular, each fixes $1$.

Composition multiplies the subscripts modulo $5$:

\[\sigma_i\circ\sigma_j=\sigma_{ij}.\]

Rigid symmetries of the regular pentagon

We can instead ask for transformations that preserve the pentagon's side lengths and adjacency. Reflections and rotations do this. Reflection across the line through $1$ is complex conjugation; rotation counterclockwise by $2\pi/5$ gives

\[1\longmapsto\zeta_5\longmapsto\zeta_5^2\longmapsto\zeta_5^3\longmapsto\zeta_5^4\longmapsto1.\]

More generally, rotating the $n$th roots by $r$ steps sends

\[R_r(\zeta_n^j)=\zeta_n^{j+r}.\]
A useful contrast

Every rotation preserves the regular polygon. A nonidentity rotation does not preserve multiplication of the roots, because it moves the multiplicative identity $1$.
